Reading the numbers

  • Cold and high-VDD corners are the hardest; warm corners are the easiest. Below 0 °C the median gate error is about 1.0 % (p95 3.2 %); above 40 °C it is about 0.6 % (p95 2.1 %). When it is cold, OFF currents are smallest and log I is most sensitive to every input, so a small error in log G becomes a larger relative error. At high VDD, DIBL is strongest, and VDD = 1.1 V is the edge of the training range. The worst gate samples all sit at two cold, high-VDD corners: −24 °C at 1.09 V and −1 °C at 1.08 V.
  • The cases with the largest tails leak through one OFF PMOS with Vsd ≈ VDD: INV 1, the AND2 cases whose output inverter sees a 1, NOR2 01 and OR2 00. The PMOS-OFF channel is the weakest of the four models. Every one of the 18 gate cases now has a bias within ±0.8 %.
  • No outliers. The old +126 % point (INV 1, corner 198) turned out to be a spurious ngspice operating point, fixed with a .nodeset (Chapter 6). The worst of the 3,600 gate points is now under 6 %.

Why the adder is less accurate than the gates, and how we fixed it

The decomposition in Chapter 8 answers this directly. Adding up SPICE gate leakages already misses the real adder by −2.6 % (median), with no ML involved. The ML part adds only about 0.7 % median error on top. The bias is negative because real logic levels are not ideal: a "0" sits a few to tens of millivolts above ground and a "1" a little below VDD. Each such input turns the next gate's OFF transistor slightly on (exponentially, through VGS), so the real circuit leaks more than a table of ideal-input gates predicts. The bias grows with temperature, from about −1 % below 0 °C to about −5 % above 80 °C, because the level shifts grow with leakage.

The proof, and the fix

When the same transistor models are run on the adder's real node voltages ("flat ML"), the error is 0.7 % median with essentially zero bias. So the remaining error belongs to the ideal-level hierarchy, not to the ML. The levels-aware hierarchy solves those real levels with the ML models instead of taking them from SPICE, and reaches the same accuracy (0.66 % median) with no SPICE input at all.
